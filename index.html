THE CRAFT

FIG. 03
THE LAND

Volcanic soil, mountain water.

The Rwenzori range holds fog most mornings and volcanic soil the rest of the year — a combination Arabica has always loved. Our trees grow in the shade of banana and native fig, at altitude, the slow way.

THE FAMILY

Three generations, one hillside.

The Nyakato family has farmed the same terraces since the 1960s. Cherries are still picked by hand, one ripe fruit at a time, by people who know every row of trees by name.

FIG. 04
"We don't rush the cherry, and we don't rush the roast. Esiimwe means gratitude — you can only earn that in a cup if you've respected everything that came before it." — Grace Nyakato, third-generation grower
FIG. 05
THE ROAST

Twelve kilos at a time.

Every batch is roasted in a twelve-kilo drum, by ear as much as by profile — listening for first crack, then holding the bean just past second before it's pulled and cooled.

FROM CHERRY TO CUP

Five steps.
No shortcuts.

01
Harvest

Only ripe, red cherries are hand-picked — the rest are left to ripen.

02
Wash

Fermented 36 hours, then washed clean in mountain spring water.

03
Dry

Sun-dried on raised beds for fourteen days, turned by hand.

04
Roast

Small-batch roasted to order, never more than two weeks before it ships.

05
Brew

Yours. Pour-over, moka, or French press — the way you take it.
